Production workflow

Placebo902 Studio is a desktop application that manages an observable, stateful path from authorized gaming media to a platform-ready post.

End-to-end stages

  1. Authorized intake. The Studio imports creator-provided gameplay media and records its source, format, duration, and fingerprint.
  2. Technical validation. Media is checked for readability, dimensions, duration, usable audio, and duplicate content.
  3. Editorial decision. The system evaluates whether the content is suitable, sufficiently distinct, and eligible to advance.
  4. Short-form production. It creates the vertical edit, adds channel branding, and applies subtitles according to the media type.
  5. Quality gates. Output is validated before it enters the approval and scheduling queue.
  6. Creator review. The creator may approve, request changes, postpone, or reject a candidate from the dashboard.
  7. Authorized publishing. Eligible media is transferred only to channels connected through their official authorization flows.
  8. Reconciliation and learning. Remote state and available analytics are synchronized so the next production decision uses current evidence.

Media-specific subtitle rules

Original narrated pieces use large, centered written narration for legibility. Creator-provided gameplay clips keep subtitles centered near the bottom so they do not obstruct the action, HUD, or important visual context.

Editorial states

StateMeaningSystem behavior
PendingCandidate is ready for checks or optional review.The pipeline can continue when all mandatory gates pass.
ApprovedCreator or policy approved the candidate.It becomes eligible for scheduling and authorized transfer.
Changes requestedThe candidate needs a correction.It is blocked until rebuilt and revalidated.
PostponedThe creator chose to defer it.It remains out of active publication selection.
RejectedThe content should not be published.It is excluded and cannot silently return to the queue.

Operational recovery

Daily health checks validate service state, storage, credentials, queue continuity, remote publication truth, analytics freshness, and disposable work files. Recoverable failures are retried or reconciled; failures that require account authorization are surfaced with a reconnect action.

The dashboard is a control and observability interface. It does not expose TikTok credentials, request the creator's TikTok password, or replace TikTok's own final draft review.
